# The Sun

The Sun is the **star** at the center of our solar system. Its gravity holds the planets and smaller bodies in orbit. Sunlight and heat make life on [Earth](/docs/astronomy/planets/earth) possible. Light from the Sun takes about **8 minutes 20 seconds** to reach Earth.

## What powers it?

The Sun is made mostly of **hydrogen and helium**. In its hot, dense core, **nuclear fusion** joins hydrogen nuclei to form helium. A small amount of mass becomes energy, which eventually escapes as light and heat. The Sun is a star, not a burning ball of fire: fusion is not chemical combustion.

## From center to space

| Region | What happens there |
|---|---|
| Core | Hydrogen fuses into helium. |
| Radiative zone | Energy moves outward mainly as radiation. |
| Convective zone | Hot plasma rises and cooler plasma sinks. |
| Photosphere | The visible surface from which most sunlight escapes. |
| Chromosphere and corona | Thin outer atmosphere; the corona extends far into space. |

The Sun's magnetic field drives **sunspots**, **flares**, and sometimes **coronal mass ejections**. It also releases a stream of charged particles called the **solar wind**. When charged particles interact with Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere, they can produce **auroras**.

## What happens next?

The Sun formed about **4.6 billion years ago**. It has several billion years of hydrogen fusion left. Later it will expand into a **red giant**, shed its outer layers, and leave a **white dwarf**. It is not massive enough to end as a supernova.

> **Safety:** Never look directly at the Sun through binoculars or a telescope without a certified solar filter fitted correctly over the front of the instrument.
