# Dwarf Planets

A dwarf planet orbits the Sun and is massive enough for gravity to make it nearly round, but it has **not cleared its orbital neighborhood** of other objects. It is not a moon. Under the International Astronomical Union's definition, dwarf planets and the eight planets are separate categories.

## Examples

| Dwarf planet | Where it orbits | Notable feature |
|---|---|---|
| Ceres | Main asteroid belt, between Mars and Jupiter | Only recognized dwarf planet in the inner solar system |
| Pluto | Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune | Has a large moon, Charon |
| Haumea | Beyond Neptune | Fast rotation gives it an elongated shape |
| Makemake | Beyond Neptune | An icy Kuiper Belt world |
| Eris | Beyond Neptune | Its discovery helped prompt a clearer planet definition |

These are the **five officially recognized dwarf planets**, not necessarily all that exist. Many more distant bodies are candidates. A dwarf planet can have moons, an atmosphere, and a complex surface; the classification describes its orbit, not how interesting it is.

> **Compare:** [Asteroids](/docs/astronomy/smaller_objects/asteroids) are often too small to become round. Ceres is round but shares its orbital zone with many other objects.
