# Asteroids

Asteroids are small bodies orbiting the Sun, mostly made of rock or metal. Many are remnants from the formation of the solar system about 4.6 billion years ago. Unlike planets, most are irregularly shaped.

## Where are they?

The **main asteroid belt** lies between [Mars](/docs/astronomy/planets/mars) and [Jupiter](/docs/astronomy/planets/jupiter). It is not a tightly packed obstacle course: asteroids are separated by enormous distances. Jupiter's gravity helped prevent the belt's material from forming a planet.

Some asteroids share a planet's orbit near stable regions called **Trojan points**. Others cross Earth's orbit; these are **near-Earth asteroids**. Astronomers track them to estimate any potential impact risk.

## What can they tell us?

- **Rocky asteroids** preserve minerals from the early solar system.
- **Metal-rich asteroids** may be fragments of bodies whose cores were exposed by collisions.
- Some asteroids contain carbon-rich materials and water-bearing minerals.

An **asteroid** is a body in space; a **meteoroid** is a smaller piece of debris. A streak of light as debris enters an atmosphere is a **meteor**; any piece that reaches the ground is a **meteorite**.

> **Compare:** [Ceres](/docs/astronomy/smaller_objects/dwarf_planets) orbits in the asteroid belt, but it is classified as a dwarf planet because gravity has made it nearly round.
