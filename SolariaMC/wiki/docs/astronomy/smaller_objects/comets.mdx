# Comets

Comets are small bodies rich in ice, dust, and rock that orbit the Sun. Far from the Sun they can look like faint, frozen nuclei. When a comet approaches the Sun, its ices release gas and dust, producing a surrounding **coma** and sometimes bright tails.

## Why do tails form?

Sunlight warms the nucleus and releases gas, which carries dust outward. Sunlight pushes dust into a broad, often curved **dust tail**. Charged particles from the Sun help form a straighter **ion tail** from ionized gas. Both generally point **away from the Sun**, not necessarily behind the comet along its path.

## Where do comets come from?

- The **Kuiper Belt**, beyond [Neptune](/docs/astronomy/planets/neptune), is a source of many short-period comets.
- The distant, roughly spherical **Oort Cloud** is the proposed source of many long-period comets. Its existence is inferred from comet orbits; it has not been directly imaged.

A comet can make repeated trips around the Sun, shedding material each time. If Earth crosses its trail of debris, tiny particles burning up in our atmosphere appear as a **meteor shower**.

> **Compare:** [Asteroids](/docs/astronomy/smaller_objects/asteroids) tend to be rockier; comets usually become especially active near the Sun because their ices release gas.
