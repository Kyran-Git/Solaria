# Moon Phases

The Moon reflects sunlight. As it orbits Earth, we see different fractions of its sunlit half. That changing view creates the phases; Earth's shadow is **not** the cause of ordinary phases. Earth's shadow falls on the Moon only during a lunar eclipse. Learn more about [The Moon](/docs/astronomy/moons/the_moon).

## One cycle

| Phase | What we see from Earth |
|---|---|
| New Moon | The near side is mostly dark; the Moon is near the Sun in our sky. |
| Waxing crescent | A growing sliver is lit. |
| First quarter | Half the visible disk is lit. |
| Waxing gibbous | More than half is lit and growing. |
| Full Moon | The visible face is nearly all lit. |
| Waning gibbous | More than half is lit and shrinking. |
| Last quarter | Half the visible disk is lit. |
| Waning crescent | A shrinking sliver is lit before the next New Moon. |

**Waxing** means the lit portion is growing; **waning** means it is shrinking. The cycle from one New Moon to the next takes about **29.5 days** (a *synodic month*), longer than the Moon's 27.3-day orbit relative to the stars. Earth travels around the Sun in the meantime, so the Moon must move a little farther to line up with the Sun again.

## Why not an eclipse every month?

The Moon's orbit is tilted about **5°** relative to Earth's orbit around the Sun. Most months the Moon passes above or below the line between Earth and Sun at New Moon, or above or below Earth's shadow at Full Moon. Only a close alignment near where the orbital planes cross produces an eclipse.

> **Try it:** Shine a lamp on a ball and walk around it. Keep half of the ball lit by the lamp, then notice how the portion you can see changes.
