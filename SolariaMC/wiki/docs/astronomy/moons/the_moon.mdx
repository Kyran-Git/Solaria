# The Moon

The Moon is a rocky world that orbits Earth. It is about 3,475 km across, roughly one-quarter of Earth's diameter. Its gravity raises tides in Earth's oceans, together with the Sun's gravity.

## Orbit and rotation

The Moon takes about **27.3 days** to orbit Earth relative to distant stars. It also takes about 27.3 days to rotate once, so the same hemisphere usually faces us. This is called **tidal locking**; the far side is not permanently dark.

Because Earth moves around the Sun during a lunar orbit, the Moon takes about **29.5 days** to return to the same phase. See [Moon Phases](/docs/astronomy/moons/moon_phases) for why its appearance changes.

## Surface and interior

- The light-colored **highlands** are old, heavily cratered crust. The dark **maria** are ancient plains of cooled basaltic lava, not seas.
- With almost no atmosphere or liquid water to erase impacts, many craters remain visible for billions of years.
- Below the crust are a rocky mantle and a small, partly molten iron-rich core. The Moon has no Earth-like global magnetic field today.

## How did it form?

The leading explanation is that a large body struck the young Earth about **4.5 billion years ago**. Debris from the collision gathered into the Moon. Lunar rocks and computer models support this **giant-impact hypothesis**, although details are still being studied.
