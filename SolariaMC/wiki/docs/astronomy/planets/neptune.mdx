## 1. Meet the Planet

- Neptune is the **8th planet** from the Sun, the **most distant** planet.
- It is the **4th largest** planet.
- It has **no hard surface**. Its hydrogen-rich atmosphere transitions into a dense, fluid interior; models differ on the nature of its innermost region.

### Ice Giant vs. Gas Giant

Neptune and Uranus are **ice giants**. Jupiter and Saturn are **gas giants**.

When the solar system formed, Neptune was **less efficient at pulling in hydrogen and helium** than Jupiter and Saturn were. As a result, its **atmosphere is much smaller compared to its core**.

| Attribute | Neptune |
|---|---|
| **Position** | 8th planet (most distant) |
| **Size rank** | 4th largest |
| **Category** | Ice giant (outer giant planet) |
| **Surface** | No solid surface beneath the clouds |
| **Chemistry** | Reduced: hydrogen, helium, water (H₂O), methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃) |

## 2. What Is Neptune Made Of?

### Reduced Chemistry

Neptune's chemistry is **reduced**, meaning it is dominated by **hydrogen and hydrogen compounds**. Nearly all of its oxygen is bound to hydrogen as **water (H₂O)**.

> **Compare:** Earth and Mars are **oxidized** (oxygen compounds like silicates). Jupiter and Neptune are **reduced** (hydrogen compounds).

### The Core

Models often include a dense inner region containing:
- **Rock**
- **Metal**
- **Ice**

## 3. Inside Neptune: Two Models

Scientists can't look inside Neptune directly, so they build **models** and test them against measurements. The best test is the **gravity data** from **Voyager 2**, which flew past Neptune in the late 1980s.

### Diagram 1: Two Models Side by Side

```
  +------------------------------+     +------------------------------+

  +------------------------------+     +------------------------------+

  |   water + methane + ammonia  |     |  lighter, stays on top       |
  |   (may include superionic    |     +------------------------------+
  |    water ice)                |     |  CARBON-RICH LAYER           |
  |                              |     |  carbon + nitrogen + hydrogen|
  |                              |     |  about 5,000 miles thick     |
  |                              |     |  heavier, sinks to bottom    |
  +------------------------------+     +------------------------------+
  |        SOLID CORE            |     |        SOLID CORE            |
  |    rock + metal + ice        |     |    rock + metal + ice        |
  +------------------------------+     +------------------------------+
```

### Model A: The Traditional Model

For a long time, scientists thought a **single, hot, highly compressed fluid layer** of **water, methane, and ammonia** surrounded the solid core.

**Superionic water ice:** At extreme temperature and pressure, the water in that layer was thought to become **superionic water**, a strange state of matter that is **hot and black** and behaves like **both a liquid and a solid** at once.

### Model B: The 2024 Layered Ocean Model

In 2024, **Burkhard Militzer** (UC Berkeley) published a study in **PNAS** using quantum-mechanical computer simulations of conditions relevant to ice-giant interiors:

| Condition | Value |
|---|---|
| Pressure | **3.4 million** times Earth's atmospheric pressure |
| Temperature | **4,750 K** (8,000°F) |

Under these conditions, the mix of water, methane, and ammonia goes through **phase separation**.

**How the layers form:**

1. **Hydrogen is squeezed out** of methane and ammonia.
2. The mixture **splits into two fluids that won't mix**, like **oil and water**.
3. **Upper layer, the global ocean:** a **water-hydrogen ocean** about **5,000 miles (8,000 km) thick**, right below the atmosphere.
4. **Lower layer, the carbon-rich layer:** a dense fluid rich in **carbon, nitrogen, and hydrogen**, also about **5,000 miles (8,000 km) thick**, right above the core.
5. **Density sorting:** the lower layer lost hydrogen, so it became **heavier** and settled to the bottom. The lighter water-hydrogen ocean floats on top. The layers **stay separate and can't convect together**.

**Does it fit the evidence?** The layered model can reproduce available gravity measurements, but Voyager 2's flyby does not uniquely determine Neptune's interior. Other models remain possible.

### Model Comparison

| Property | Traditional model | 2024 layered model |
|---|---|---|
| **Mantle structure** | One uniform, mixed fluid | Two separate, unmixable fluid layers |
| **Upper layer** | Water, methane, ammonia mixture | Water-hydrogen ocean (about 5,000 miles) |
| **Lower layer** | None (uniform mixture to the core) | Carbon-nitrogen-hydrogen fluid (about 5,000 miles) |
| **Key physics** | Superionic water ice and diamond rain | Layers separate because hydrogen is lost under extreme pressure |
| **Convection** | Depends on the model | Could be limited by the separation between layers |
| **Magnetic field origin** | Complex motions of superionic water | Convection in the upper ocean only, explaining the odd field shape |
| **Voyager 2 match** | Some models fit available constraints | One possible fit to gravity and magnetic-field observations |

## 4. Diamond Rain

Deep inside ice giants like Neptune, the pressure is so extreme that **carbon atoms can be squeezed into solid diamond**. The idea is that diamonds then **sink ("rain down") through the interior**.

> **Important:** Diamond rain has been a **theory** for a long time. The 2024 layered model can explain Neptune's gravity and magnetic behavior **without needing diamond rain or superionic water**. Scientists are still working out which picture is right.

## 5. Atmosphere and Storms

### What the Air Is Made Of

Neptune has a **deep, thick atmosphere** over its fluid interior:

| Gas | Formula |
|---|---|
| Molecular hydrogen | H₂ |
| Helium | He |
| Methane | CH₄ |
| Ammonia | NH₃ |

Gases leave **"fingerprints"** in spectra. Methane is detected in Neptune's atmosphere; ammonia is much harder to observe in its upper atmosphere and is expected deeper down.

### The Great Dark Spot

The Hubble Space Telescope and spacecraft flybys have shown that Neptune's atmosphere is **active and stormy**.

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **First seen** | By **Voyager 2** during its **1989** flyby |
| **Compared to Jupiter's Great Red Spot** | Both are large atmospheric vortices, but Neptune's observed dark spots can appear and disappear much faster |
| **Fate** | By **1994**, it had **completely disappeared** |
| **Afterwards** | A **similar dark spot appeared farther north** |

## 6. Neptune's Strange Magnetic Field

### Dipole vs. Disorganized

Voyager 2 found that Neptune's magnetic field is **strongly tilted and offset** from the planet's center, with significant non-dipole components. It still has a dipole component; it is not a planet without magnetic poles.

### Diagram 2: Earth vs. Neptune

```
  EARTH (near-centered dipole)       NEPTUNE (tilted, offset field)

        [ N POLE ]                          [ variable, off-center ]
           |||                                 /   \   \
    <----> ||| <---->                         /     \   \
    Convection in Earth's             A conducting fluid shell is
    liquid outer core                 one possible dynamo location;
           |||                        Neptune's interior is uncertain
        [ S POLE ]
```

### Why Is It Disorganized?

1. **Earth:** Convection in the liquid outer core is **global**, so it makes a **clean dipole field**.
2. **Neptune:** Its tilted, offset field suggests a different dynamo geometry from Earth's.
3. **The barrier:** In the layered model, the boundary between the **water-rich ocean** and the **carbon-rich layer** **blocks convection** from spanning the whole interior.
4. **Possible shell dynamo:** A conducting fluid shell could generate an unusual magnetic field. The depth and makeup of that shell are still uncertain.

> **Compare:** Uranus and Neptune both have tilted, offset magnetic fields. Layered interior models are possible explanations, not confirmed maps of either planet.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ice giant** | A giant planet with a small atmosphere compared to a large core of rock, metal, and ice (Uranus, Neptune) |
| **Gas giant** | A giant planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium (Jupiter, Saturn) |
| **Reduced chemistry** | Dominated by hydrogen and hydrogen compounds |
| **Superionic water** | Hot, black water that acts like both a liquid and a solid |
| **Phase separation** | A mixture splitting into layers that won't mix, like oil and water |
| **Immiscible** | Unable to mix |
| **Diamond rain** | Theory that carbon is squeezed into diamond and sinks through the interior |
| **Dipole field** | Magnetic field with two opposite poles |
| **Non-dipole components** | Parts of a magnetic field not described by a simple bar magnet |
| **Convection** | Heat carried by moving fluid |
| **Spectroscopy** | Studying light's "fingerprint" to identify gases |

## Review Questions

1. Why is Neptune an ice giant instead of a gas giant?
2. What chemical state describes Neptune, and where does most of its oxygen end up?
3. What did the traditional model say about Neptune's mantle?
4. In the 2024 layered model, how many layers are there above the core, and what are they?
5. What causes the layers to separate, and why don't they mix?
6. What was the Great Dark Spot, and what happened to it?
7. How does Neptune's magnetic field differ from Earth's?

### Answers

1. It pulled in less hydrogen and helium than Jupiter or Saturn during formation, so it has a much smaller atmosphere compared to its rock, metal, and ice core.
2. Reduced chemistry (dominated by hydrogen). Nearly all its oxygen is bound with hydrogen as water (H₂O).
3. A single, hot, compressed fluid layer of water, methane, and ammonia around the core, possibly including superionic water.
4. Two: a water-hydrogen ocean on top and a carbon-rich layer below, each about 5,000 miles thick.
5. Extreme pressure (3.4 million atm) and heat (4,750 K) squeeze hydrogen out of methane and ammonia. The heavier carbon-rich fluid sinks and the lighter water-hydrogen ocean stays on top, like oil and water.
6. A large atmospheric vortex Voyager 2 saw in 1989. It had disappeared by 1994, and a different dark spot was later observed farther north.
7. Neptune's field is strongly tilted and offset, unlike Earth's near-centered dipole. A dynamo in a conducting shell is one possible explanation; its exact interior is uncertain.
