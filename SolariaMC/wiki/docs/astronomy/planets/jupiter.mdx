## 1. Meet the Planet

Jupiter is the **largest planet** in the solar system and the classic **gas giant**. It has **no solid surface to stand on**. Its makeup is close to that of the **Sun** and the cloud of gas and dust the solar system formed from (the *protosolar nebula*).

### What Is It Made Of?

| Ingredient | Share of Jupiter's mass |
|---|---|
| **Hydrogen** | Most of its mass (roughly three-quarters) |
| **Helium** | Most of the remainder, plus a small fraction of heavier elements |

In the atmosphere, hydrogen (H₂) and helium (He) are present in roughly the same proportions as in the Sun. The helium mass fraction is **0.234**, a little **lower** than the original solar value. (Section 2 explains why.)

### Reduced Chemistry

Jupiter's chemistry is **reduced**, meaning it is dominated by **hydrogen and hydrogen compounds**. Almost all of its oxygen is locked up in **water (H₂O)**. Other simple gases in the deep atmosphere:

| Gas | Formula |
|---|---|
| Methane | CH₄ |
| Ammonia | NH₃ |
| Hydrogen sulfide | H₂S |
| Phosphine | PH₃ |
| Arsine and germane (trace amounts) | AsH₃, GeH₄ |

> **Compare:** Earth and Mars are **oxidized** (oxygen compounds like silicates). Jupiter is **reduced** (hydrogen compounds).

### Jupiter vs. the Sun: Measured Abundances

In **December 1995**, the **Galileo probe** dove into Jupiter's atmosphere. Together with data from the Infrared Space Observatory, Cassini, and ground telescopes, it showed that many **heavy elements** in the deep atmosphere (below 10 bar) are **more abundant than in the Sun**.

| Element | Compared to the Sun |
|---|---|
| Helium | Slightly depleted (0.807x) |
| **Neon** | **Severely depleted (0.1x)** |
| Argon | Enriched (2.5x) |
| Krypton | Enriched (2.7x) |
| Xenon | Enriched (2.6x) |
| Carbon | Enriched (2.9x) |
| Nitrogen | Enriched (about 3.2 to 3.6x) |
| Sulfur | Enriched (2.5x) |
| Deuterium/Hydrogen | Matches the original protosolar value |
| Nitrogen isotope ratio (¹⁵N/¹⁴N) | About 1/3 lower than Earth's (Jupiter: 2.3 × 10⁻³, Earth: 3.5 × 10⁻³) |

> **Vocabulary:** One **bar** is roughly the air pressure at sea level on Earth. Scientists use pressure instead of height on Jupiter because there is no surface to measure from.

## 2. Inside Jupiter

Deep inside Jupiter, temperatures and pressures are far beyond the critical points of hydrogen and helium. So there is **no sharp boundary** between the "atmosphere" and the "interior." Gas just gets denser until it behaves like a liquid.

### Diagram 1: Jupiter's Interior (Top to Center)

```
  OUTER ATMOSPHERE / CLOUD DECKS
  (1 bar = the "zero point" for measuring altitude)
  ====================================================
  |  MOLECULAR HYDROGEN (H2)                          |
  |  Gas that becomes supercritical fluid with depth  |
  |---------------- about 10,000 km deep -------------|
  |  LIQUID METALLIC HYDROGEN MANTLE                  |
  |  Electrically conductive liquid                   |
  |  * Helium droplets form and fall ("helium rain")  |
  |  * Neon dissolves into the falling droplets       |
  |---------------------------------------------------|
  |  DENSE CORE (inferred from gravity)               |
  |  Rock, metal, and water / ammonia / methane ice   |
  ====================================================
  CENTER OF JUPITER
```

### The Core and Differentiation

- Studies of Jupiter's gravity suggest a **dense central core** of **rock, metal, and ices** (water, ammonia, methane).
- As with rocky planets, **gravity pulled heavy material toward the center** during formation while light gases stayed in the outer layers.

### Liquid Metallic Hydrogen

- Jupiter's gravity squeezes hydrogen gas into a **dense liquid** as depth increases.
- Below about **10,000 km**, the pressure is so extreme that electrons break free from their protons. This is **liquid metallic hydrogen**.
- Because the electrons move freely, the fluid **conducts electricity**.

### Helium Rain

1. Where molecular hydrogen turns into metallic hydrogen (deeper than about 10,000 km), **helium no longer mixes** with the hydrogen.
2. The helium **condenses into droplets**.
3. The droplets are **denser** than the surrounding liquid metallic hydrogen, so they **sink toward the core**. This is **helium rain**.
4. The rain **removes helium** from the upper atmosphere. That is why Jupiter's helium fraction (0.234) is **below** the original solar value.
5. **Neon dissolves easily into helium droplets**, so the falling rain **drags neon downward**. That explains Jupiter's severe neon shortage (0.1x solar).

### Jupiter's Energy Budget

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Emitted heat vs. absorbed sunlight | **1.67 : 1** (more heat out than sunlight in) |
| Internal heat flux | 5.44 W/m² |
| Total thermal power | 335 PW (about one-billionth of the Sun's output) |

**Where does the extra heat come from?**
- **Leftover formation heat** from Jupiter's gravitational collapse about **4.5 billion years ago**.
- **Gravitational energy** released as helium sinks may contribute to its heat output.

**Even temperatures:** The poles get much less sunlight than the equator, yet the **convecting interior spreads heat around**, so the troposphere stays at **nearly the same temperature from equator to poles**.

## 3. Layers of the Atmosphere

Jupiter's atmosphere has **no clear bottom**. It blends smoothly into the fluid interior. Scientists set **0 km at the 1 bar pressure level**.

### Diagram 2: Atmospheric Layers

```
  ALTITUDE      PRESSURE       LAYER
  +5000 km  --- near-vacuum -- EXOSPHERE
                               Fades into interplanetary space
  +1000 km  --- 1 nbar ------- THERMOSPHERE
                               800-1000 K, auroral H3+ glow
  +320 km   --- 1 microbar --- STRATOSPHERE
                               Warms to ~200 K, hydrocarbon hazes
  +50 km    --- 0.1 bar ------ TROPOPAUSE
                               Coldest point: ~110 K
     0 km   --- 1 bar -------- TROPOSPHERE
                               Weather and clouds (ammonia ice at 0.6-0.9 bar)
  -90 km    --- 10 bar ------- BASE OF TROPOSPHERE
                               About 340 K
```

### The Four Layers

| Layer | Range | What happens here |
|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | 10 bar (-90 km) up to 0.1 bar (+50 km) | Moist convection, clouds, storms. Temperature falls from about 340 K to a minimum of about 110 K |
| **Stratosphere** | 0.1 bar (+50 km) to 1 microbar (+320 km) | Temperature rises from 110 K to about 200 K by absorbing solar ultraviolet (UV) light |
| **Thermosphere** | 1 microbar (+320 km) to 1 nbar (about +1000 km) | Extremely hot: 800 to 1000 K |
| **Exosphere** | About 1000 km out to about 5000 km | Merges with interplanetary space |

> **Compare to Earth:** Earth has **five** layers, including a mesosphere. **Jupiter has no mesosphere.**

### Three Cloud Decks

Clouds form at different heights because different substances **condense at different temperatures**.

| Deck | Pressure | Made of | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper** | 0.6 to 0.9 bar | **Ammonia ice** (NH₃) | White crystals |
| **Middle** | 1 to 2 bar | **Ammonium hydrosulfide** (NH₄SH) or ammonium sulfide ((NH₄)₂S) | Crystals |
| **Lower** | 3 to 7 bar | **Water ice** (H₂O) and liquid droplets | Densest deck. Strongest effect on weather because water releases lots of heat as it condenses |

**No methane clouds:** Methane (CH₄) never condenses on Jupiter because the troposphere is **too warm** for it.

### Chemistry and Hazes in the Upper Atmosphere

- **Hydrocarbons:** Solar UV light and charged particles from Jupiter's magnetosphere **break apart methane**. This produces **ethane (C₂H₆)**, **acetylene (C₂H₂)**, and **diacetylene (C₄H₂)**.
- **Hazes:** Stratospheric hazes (between 1 microbar and 100 mbar) are made of **polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)** or **hydrazine**.
- **External debris:** Some **carbon dioxide (CO₂)**, **carbon monoxide (CO)**, and **stratospheric water** came from impacts by small icy bodies. Water from below can't rise past the **tropopause**, which acts as a **cold trap** (about 110 K).

## 4. Bands, Belts, and Jet Streams

Jupiter's visible surface is arranged in **stripes parallel to the equator**, separated by fast **jet streams**.

### Zones vs. Belts

| | **Zones** | **Belts** |
|---|---|---|
| Color | Light | Dark |
| Temperature | Cooler | Warmer |
| Air motion | **Upwelling** (rising) | **Downwelling** (sinking) |
| Why it looks that way | Rising ammonia-rich gas cools and forms high, dense white ammonia ice clouds | Sinking gas warms and clears the ammonia clouds, exposing deeper, darker clouds colored by sulfur, phosphorus, and carbon compounds |

### Diagram 3: Bands and Jets

```
  JET DIRECTION                          BAND
  =====================================  North Temperate Belt (NTB)
                                         dark, warm, sinking air
  <--- Retrograde jet (westward) <---
  -------------------------------------  North Tropical Zone (NTropZ)
                                         light, cold, rising air
  ---> Prograde jet (eastward) --->
  =====================================  North Equatorial Belt (NEB)
                                         dark, active cyclones
```

### Jet Stream Rules

- **Prograde (eastward) jets:** at the edge where a **zone changes to a belt**, moving away from the equator.
- **Retrograde (westward) jets:** at the edge where a **belt changes to a zone**.
- **Equatorial Zone exception (7°S to 7°N):** an extra-powerful prograde jet over **100 m/s (390 km/h)**.

### Deep Circulation Cells (Juno Discovery)

NASA's **Juno** spacecraft used its **Microwave Radiometer (MWR)** to look under the clouds.

- There are **eight closed circulation cells per hemisphere**, between **20° and 60° N/S**.
- They reach from **1 bar down to at least 240 bar**.
- Air **rises in belts** and **sinks in zones** across these deep layers.
- **Breaking Rossby waves** drive the cells. They work like Jupiter's version of Earth's **Ferrel cells**.

### Two Models of What Drives the Jets

Scientists have two competing explanations.

| | **Shallow Model** | **Deep Model** |
|---|---|---|
| Main driver | Moist convection in the weather layer above the water clouds (3 to 7 bar) | Heat flowing up from the core through the whole mantle |
| Mechanism | 2D turbulence merges small vortices into bigger ones (an "inverse cascade") until they reach the **Rhines scale** | The **Taylor-Proudman theorem**: fast rotation arranges fluid flow into **cylinders parallel to the spin axis** |
| Strengths | Makes narrow, stable jets that match the real band widths | Naturally makes strong prograde equatorial jets and long-term stability |
| Weaknesses | Struggles to make prograde equatorial jets, and predicts unstable retrograde flow at the equator | Simulations historically produced too few, overly wide jets |

**Evidence from Galileo:** Wind speeds **did not weaken** below the cloud tops. They stayed the same down to at least **22 bar**, which suggests **deep mechanisms matter**.

## 5. Storms and Weather Features

### The Great Red Spot (GRS)

The **Great Red Spot** is a giant **anticyclone** (a high-pressure storm) at **22°S**.

```
         RETROGRADE (WESTWARD) JET to the north
  ===========================================
              /-----------------\
             /   Cool cloudtops  \
            |  (about +8 km high) |
   <---     |    /-----------\    |     <---
            |   |  Warm core  |   |
            |   |  (+3 to 4 K)|   |
            |    \-----------/    |
             \                   /
              \-----------------/
  ===========================================
         PROGRADE (EASTWARD) JET to the south

   Spins counter-clockwise, one turn every ~6 Earth days
```

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Tracked continuously since the **1870s**. Observations go back to **Cassini (1665)** and **Bandtius/Riccioli (1635)** |
| **Rotation** | Counter-clockwise, about **6 Earth days** per turn (14 Jovian days) |
| **Size** | 24,000 to 40,000 km east-west and 12,000 to 14,000 km north-south, large enough to hold **2 to 3 Earths**. Its long axis **shrank 15%** between 1996 and 2006 |
| **Height** | Cloudtops are about **8 km higher** than the surroundings and are **colder** |
| **Warm center** | Its central red region is **3 to 4 K warmer** than its surroundings (at 200 to 500 mbar) because of weak sinking air |
| **Winds** | Up to **120 m/s (430 km/h)** at the edges |
| **Color** | Possibly from chemicals broken apart by UV light, organic molecules, red phosphorus, or sulfur compounds |

The GRS sits **between** a strong retrograde jet to the north and a modest prograde jet to the south.

### Oval BA ("Red Spot Jr.")

- **Formed in March 2000** when three white ovals (**FA, BC, and DE**, all dating back to **1939**) **merged** in the South Temperate Belt.
- **Turned red** between **August 2005 and February 2006**, as its winds sped up to **618 km/h** and pulled up gases that UV light then changed.
- **Lost its red color** by late **2018**.

### Convective Storms, "Mushballs," and Lightning

- **Storm plumes** rise over **100 km** from the water cloud base (5 to 7 bar) up to **0.2 to 0.5 bar**.
- **Lightning** is **hundreds of times more energetic** than Earth's. Juno saw **shallow lightning** at 1.1 to 1.5 bar, above the water clouds.
- **Mushballs:** Strong storms lift ammonia vapor, which **dissolves into water ice** to make **slushy ammonia-water hail**. These **mushballs** fall deep into the atmosphere and carry ammonia down with them.

### Hot Spots and Festoons

**Hot spots** sit mostly along the **northern edge of the Equatorial Zone**. They are **cloud-free windows** where heat from the warm deep atmosphere escapes. They glow brightly in **5 micrometer infrared** light. **Large cloudy plumes**, up to **10,000 km across**, border them on the west.

### Polar Cyclone Clusters (Juno Discovery)

Each pole has a **central cyclone** surrounded by a **stable ring of smaller cyclones**.

| Pole | Pattern | Central cyclone radius |
|---|---|---|
| **North** | **8** cyclones in an **octagon** | 4,000 to 4,600 km |
| **South** | **5** cyclones in a **pentagon** | 5,600 to 7,000 km |

**Why the shapes stay stable:** **Beta drift** pushes cyclones **toward the pole**, while the **central cyclone repels** them. The two forces balance.

## 6. Magnetism, Auroras, and High-Energy Physics

Jupiter's upper atmosphere is shaped by its **powerful magnetosphere**.

### Diagram 4: Energy Flow in the Upper Atmosphere

```
  Charged particles trapped in Jupiter's magnetosphere
                        |
                        v
          Particles pour into the polar atmosphere
                        |
      +-----------------+------------------------------+
      |                 |                              |
      v                 v                              v
  Permanent         X-ray emission             Joule heating + ion drag
  auroral ovals     (first seen by the         (solves the thermosphere
                    Einstein Observatory,       "energy crisis")
                    1983)                              |
                                                       v
                                         H3+ ions radiate infrared light
                                         at 2 to 5 micrometers
                                         (main cooling mechanism)

  Also: UV and charged particles turn methane (CH4)
        into ethane (C2H6) and acetylene (C2H2)
```

### Permanent Auroras and X-Rays

- On Earth, auroras appear mainly during **temporary magnetic storms**. Jupiter's polar auroras are **permanent**, because charged particles pour into the polar atmosphere **all the time**.
- Jupiter's polar upper atmosphere also **emits X-rays**, first detected by the **Einstein Observatory in 1983**.

### The Thermosphere "Energy Crisis"

| | Temperature |
|---|---|
| **Predicted** from solar UV heating alone | No more than about **400 K** |
| **Actually observed** | **800 to 1000 K** |

**The solution:** Magnetospheric energy causes **Joule heating**, frictional heating from electric currents in the ionosphere, mostly near the poles. **Upper-atmosphere winds and ion drag** carry that heat toward lower latitudes.

### H₃⁺ Cooling

- Jupiter's thermosphere was the **first place outside Earth** where the **trihydrogen cation (H₃⁺)** was detected.
- H₃⁺ **radiates infrared light at 2 to 5 micrometers**, which is the **main way the thermosphere cools**. This stops the temperature from running away.

## Quick Comparison: Jupiter vs. Earth

| Property | Earth | Jupiter |
|---|---|---|
| Planet class | Terrestrial (rocky) | Gas giant |
| Surface | Solid | None |
| Main material | Silicates and iron | Hydrogen and helium |
| Chemistry | Oxidized | Reduced |
| Atmosphere layers | 5 (includes mesosphere) | 4 (no mesosphere) |
| Magnetic field source | Liquid iron outer core | Liquid metallic hydrogen |
| Internal heat | About half from radioactive decay | Formation heat plus helium rain |
| Nitrogen isotope ratio (¹⁵N/¹⁴N) | 3.5 × 10⁻³ | 2.3 × 10⁻³ |

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Gas giant** | A huge planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium with no solid surface |
| **Reduced chemistry** | Dominated by hydrogen and hydrogen compounds |
| **Bar** | A unit of pressure, about equal to Earth's sea-level air pressure |
| **Liquid metallic hydrogen** | Hydrogen squeezed so hard that its electrons move freely, making it conduct electricity |
| **Helium rain** | Helium droplets sinking through metallic hydrogen |
| **Tropopause** | The coldest boundary between troposphere and stratosphere (about 110 K) |
| **Cold trap** | A cold layer that freezes out rising vapor so it cannot pass |
| **Zone** | Light, cool band where air rises |
| **Belt** | Dark, warm band where air sinks |
| **Prograde / Retrograde** | Flowing eastward / westward |
| **Anticyclone** | A high-pressure storm, like the Great Red Spot |
| **Aurora** | Glow caused by charged particles hitting the upper atmosphere |
| **Joule heating** | Heating from electric current flowing through a material |
