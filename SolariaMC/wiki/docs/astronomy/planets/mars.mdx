## 1. Meet the Planet

- Mars is the **4th planet from the Sun**.
- It is the **7th largest** planet in the solar system.
- It is a **terrestrial planet** (also called *inner* or *rocky*), like Mercury, Venus, and Earth. That means it has a **solid surface** instead of a thick envelope of gas.

### Mars's Neighborhood

Mars orbits just **inside the main asteroid belt**. The belt separates the inner rocky planets from the outer gas and ice giants. It holds objects like **Ceres**, the **only dwarf planet** in the inner solar system.

```
  Sun  >  Mercury  >  Venus  >  Earth  >  MARS  >  [ASTEROID BELT]  >  outer giants
                                                          |
                                                        Ceres
```

## 2. What Is Mars Made Of?

### Bulk Composition

Mars is built from the same basic ingredients as Earth and Venus.

| Material | Share of Mars's mass | Made of |
|---|---|---|
| **Metals** | About **1/3** | Iron-nickel or iron-sulfur |
| **Silicates** | About **2/3** | Silicon + oxygen minerals |

### Oxidized Chemistry

Mars is mostly **oxygen compounds**, especially **silicate minerals** in its crust and outer layers. Scientists call this **oxidized chemistry**.

### Samples From Mars

We don't have to rely only on telescopes and spacecraft. **Pieces of Mars have landed on Earth as meteorites.** Labs can study them directly, which gives scientists real Martian rock to test.

## 3. Inside Mars

### Differentiation: How Mars Got Layers

**Differentiation** is the gravitational sorting of a planet's interior into layers by **density**.

1. **Metal sinks.** Dense iron-nickel and iron-sulfur sank toward the center and formed the **core**.
2. **Silicates float.** Lighter silicate minerals rose and formed the **crust** and upper layers.
3. **Layers lock in.** As Mars cooled, the layered structure was preserved.

> **Think of it like oil and water:** shake them up, wait, and the heavy liquid settles to the bottom.

### The Heat Requirement

For this sorting to happen, Mars had to be **molten early in its history**. A rocky planet must be heated **above the melting point of rock**, typically **above 1300 K**, so that heavy and light materials can move past each other.

## 4. Atmosphere and Climate History

### Why Atmosphere Matters

A solid planet's temperature depends heavily on its atmosphere and how well it **insulates** (the **greenhouse effect**).

| Planet | Atmosphere effect |
|---|---|
| **Earth** | Keeps surface temperatures mostly between water's freezing and boiling points |
| **Venus** | Dense carbon dioxide atmosphere traps extreme heat |
| **Mars** | Atmosphere density controls how much heat it keeps |

### A Warmer Mars?

- Scientific models suggest Mars **may have had a much larger, thicker atmosphere** long ago.
- A thicker atmosphere would have **insulated** the planet better.
- That could have supported a **far more temperate climate** than the cold Mars we see today.

> **Big idea:** Atmosphere thickness can change a planet's climate dramatically.

## Quick Comparison

| Property | Mars |
|---|---|
| Position | 4th planet from the Sun (inner solar system) |
| Size rank | 7th largest planet |
| Planet class | Terrestrial / rocky |
| Bulk composition | ~1/3 iron-nickel/iron-sulfur, ~2/3 silicates |
| Chemical state | Oxidized |
| Interior | Differentiated: dense metal core, silicate crust |
| Differentiation temperature | Above rock's melting point (>1300 K) |
| Past climate | Possibly temperate under a larger atmosphere |
| Direct samples | Martian meteorites |

Mars also has polar ice caps, a thin carbon dioxide atmosphere, and ancient valley networks and lake deposits. These landforms are evidence that liquid water flowed on its surface in the past. Whether Mars ever hosted life remains an open question.
