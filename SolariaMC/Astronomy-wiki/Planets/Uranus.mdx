# Uranus: The Tilted Ice Giant

*Atmosphere, interior models, diamond rain, and a disorganized magnetic field*

**What you will learn:**
- Where Uranus sits and why it is an *ice giant*
- What its atmosphere is made of
- Two competing models of its interior
- What "diamond rain" is
- Why its magnetic field is so unusual

---

## 1. Meet the Planet

- Uranus is the **7th planet** from the Sun.
- It is the **3rd largest** planet.
- It is an **ice giant**, a separate class from the **gas giants** (Jupiter and Saturn) and the **terrestrial planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars).

### What Makes an Ice Giant?

- **No solid surface.** Uranus has a deep atmosphere of swirling gas that blends into a dense interior around a central core.
- **Small atmosphere compared to its core.** Uranus is much smaller than Jupiter and Saturn. When the solar system formed, it was **less efficient at pulling in hydrogen and helium**. So its gas atmosphere is small compared to its core.
- **A core of rock, metal, and ice** sits beneath the atmosphere and mantle layers.

---

## 2. Atmosphere and Chemistry

Uranus's atmosphere is **reduced**, meaning it is dominated by **hydrogen and hydrogen compounds**.

| Gas | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular hydrogen | H₂ | Main component |
| Helium | He | Main component |
| **Methane** | CH₄ | Confirmed by spectroscopy. A defining part of the deep atmosphere |
| Ammonia | NH₃ | Present |
| Water | H₂O | Abundant. Nearly all of the planet's oxygen is bound to hydrogen as water, mostly ice in the outer solar system |

## 3. Inside Uranus

Conditions deep inside are extreme.

| Condition | Estimated value |
|---|---|
| Pressure | **3.4 million** times Earth's atmospheric pressure |
| Temperature | **4,750 K** (8,000 °F) |

Scientists use **two models** for the interior.

### Model A: The Traditional Superionic Fluid Model

- **One hot, dense fluid** of icy water, methane, and ammonia surrounds the solid core.
- **Superionic water:** under extreme heat and pressure, water may become a **hot, dense, "black ice"** that is **part solid and part liquid**.

### Model B: The 2024 Phase-Separated Layered Ocean Model

In 2024, **Militzer and colleagues (UC Berkeley)** used computer modeling to suggest that Uranus's interior splits into **separate layers that don't mix**, like **oil and water**.

1. **Upper water-hydrogen ocean:** a global ocean of water and hydrogen about **5,000 miles (8,000 km) thick**, right below the thick atmosphere.
2. **Lower carbon-rich layer:** a compressed fluid rich in **carbon, nitrogen, and hydrogen**, also about **5,000 miles (8,000 km) thick**, below the water ocean.
3. **How it forms:** extreme conditions **squeeze hydrogen out of methane and ammonia**. The lower material loses hydrogen, becomes **denser**, and settles at the bottom. The **lighter water layer stays on top**, so the two regions don't mix.

## 4. Diamond Rain

One well-known hypothesis about ice giant interiors is **diamond rain**:

1. **Break apart:** deep in the atmosphere and upper mantle, extreme pressure and temperature **break apart hydrocarbons** such as methane.
2. **Squeeze:** the pressure **squeezes isolated carbon atoms into crystalline diamond**.
3. **Fall:** the diamonds are **denser than the surrounding gas and fluid**, so they **rain down** toward the deeper interior.

> **Scientific status:** Diamond rain has long been proposed to help explain internal energy and material movement. But the two-layer model suggests that **fluid layering alone** can explain the interior **without diamond precipitation**.

## 5. A Disorganized Magnetic Field

**Voyager 2** flew past Uranus and found an **unusual magnetosphere**.

### No Dipole

| Planet | Magnetic field |
|---|---|
| Earth, Jupiter, Saturn | **Structured dipole** (like a bar magnet) |
| **Uranus** | **Non-dipolar and highly disorganized** |

### Why? Convection Is Limited

- **On Earth,** a dipole field comes from **convection** (heat moving through fluid) in the **liquid outer iron core**.
- **No dipole on Uranus** suggests there is **no planet-wide, deep convection** through the whole interior.

### The Layered Ocean Explanation

```
  EARTH (dipole)                       URANUS (disorganized)

        [ N POLE ]                       [ variable, off-center ]
           |||                              /   \   \
    <----> ||| <---->                      /     \   \
   Global convection                  Convection ONLY in the upper
   through the liquid                 water-hydrogen ocean
   outer core                         ---------------------------
           |||                          Stable carbon-rich layer
        [ S POLE ]                      (convection blocked)
```

1. The **heavy carbon layer sits stably under the lighter water layer** without mixing.
2. That **blocks planet-wide convection**.
3. Convection happens **only locally** in the upper **5,000-mile** water-hydrogen ocean.
4. This **localized convection** makes Uranus's complicated, disorganized field and **prevents a global dipole**.

## Review Questions

1. What makes Uranus an ice giant instead of a gas giant?
2. Which gases make up Uranus's atmosphere, and which one is confirmed by spectroscopy as a defining deep-atmosphere component?
3. How do the two interior models differ?
4. Describe diamond rain in three steps.
5. Why doesn't Uranus have a dipole magnetic field?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. It pulled in less hydrogen and helium than Jupiter and Saturn, so it has a much smaller atmosphere compared to its rock, metal, and ice core.
2. Hydrogen and helium are the main gases, with methane (confirmed by spectroscopy), ammonia, and water also present.
3. The traditional model has one hot, mixed fluid layer (possibly with superionic water). The 2024 model has two non-mixing layers, a water-hydrogen ocean over a carbon-rich layer.
4. Extreme pressure breaks apart methane, squeezes carbon into diamond, and the dense diamonds sink toward the interior.
5. Planet-wide convection doesn't occur. In the layered model, convection is limited to the upper water-hydrogen ocean, producing a disorganized field.
