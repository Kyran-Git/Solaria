# Earth: A Living Planet

*Physical characteristics, dynamics, and habitability*

**What you will learn:**
- Where Earth sits in the solar system and what kind of planet it is
- How Earth and the Moon move, and why a "month" has two lengths
- What Earth is made of inside, and why it has layers
- Why Earth has a magnetic field
- How the atmosphere is organized and why Earth's surface looks young
- Why Earth can support life

---

## 1. Meet the Planet

Earth is the **third planet from the Sun**. Our solar system has **8 official planets** and **5 dwarf planets**.

Earth is a **terrestrial planet** (also called an *inner* or *rocky* planet). That means it has a **solid surface** made mostly of rock and metal. The giant outer planets, like Jupiter, are made mostly of gas and ice and have no solid surface to stand on.

### Size and Scale

- Earth is the **5th largest** planet.
- Jupiter's diameter is about **11 times** Earth's.
- Earth's diameter is about **4 times** that of Europa, one of Jupiter's moons.

### Oxidized Chemistry

Earth is **oxidized**. Its rocks are mostly **oxygen compounds**, such as **silicates** (silicon + oxygen). Think of sand and granite. Most of Earth's solid material is built on oxygen.

### Earth vs. Jupiter

| Property | Earth | Compared to Jupiter |
|---|---|---|
| Planet type | Terrestrial (rocky) | Jupiter is a gas giant |
| Surface gravity | 1x (baseline) | Jupiter's is about 2.5x stronger |
| Atmosphere weight (molecular weight) | 1x (baseline) | Earth's is about 15x heavier |
| Adiabatic lapse rate | About 9.8 °C per km | Set by gravity and atmosphere weight |
| Nitrogen isotope ratio (¹⁵N/¹⁴N) | 3.5 × 10⁻³ | Higher than Jupiter's 2.3 × 10⁻³ |

> **Vocabulary:** The **adiabatic lapse rate** is how fast air cools as it rises. Climb 1 km and rising air cools by roughly 9.8 °C.

## 2. Orbits, Days, and Months

Earth **spins** on its axis (rotation) while also **traveling around the Sun** (revolution). Because both happen at once, there are **two ways** to measure a day or a month:

1. **Sidereal**: measured against the **background stars**.
2. **Synodic (solar)**: measured against the **Sun**, such as the repeating cycle of Moon phases.

### Why the Moon Takes Extra Time

The Moon orbits Earth about once a month. But while it orbits, **Earth is also moving around the Sun**. So after one full 360° lap, the Moon has to travel a bit **farther** to line up with the Sun again.

| Month type | What it measures | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Sidereal month** | One full orbit of Earth, relative to the stars | **27.3 days** |
| **Synodic month** | One full cycle of phases (New Moon to New Moon) | **29.53 days** |

### Moonrise Shift

The Moon moves along its orbit as Earth spins. Because of that, the Moon rises about **50 minutes later each day**.

> **Quick check:** Which month is longer, sidereal or synodic? Why?
> *(Answer: synodic, because the Earth-Moon system has moved around the Sun, so the Moon must travel farther to realign.)*

## 3. Inside Earth

### Age

Earth formed about **4.5 billion years ago**. Earth formed **before** the Moon.

### What Earth Is Made Of

| Part | Share of Earth's mass | Made of |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron-metal portion** | About **1/3** | Iron-nickel or iron-sulfur |
| **Silicate portion** | About **2/3** | Silicon + oxygen minerals |

Iron is the **most abundant metal** on Earth.

### Differentiation: How Earth Got Layers

**Differentiation** is the process that sorted Earth's insides by density.

1. **Heat it up.** A rocky body must be heated above the melting point of rock, typically **above 1300 K**.
2. **Let gravity sort it.** When Earth was molten, **dense iron sank** to the center, and **light silicates floated** toward the top.
3. **Freeze it in place.** As Earth cooled, the layers locked in.

```
  SURFACE CRUST   light silicates (oxygen + silicon)
        |
        v
  MANTLE          medium-density silicates
        |
        v
  CORE            heavy metals (iron-nickel / iron-sulfur)
```

> **Think of it like oil and water:** shake them up, wait, and the heavier liquid settles to the bottom.

### Earth's Heat Engine

Earth still holds a lot of interior heat, and that heat powers volcanoes, earthquakes, and moving plates.

- About **half** of Earth's current internal heat comes from **radioactive decay** inside the planet.
- That decay constantly releases energy as heat.

### Radioactive Clocks

Some atoms are unstable and slowly turn into other atoms. The **half-life** is the time it takes for **half** of a sample to decay. Scientists compare how much **parent** isotope is left to how much **daughter** isotope has built up. That ratio tells them how old a rock is.

| Parent isotope | Daughter isotope | Half-life (billion years) |
|---|---|---|
| Samarium-147 | Neodymium-143 | 106 |
| Rubidium-87 | Strontium-87 | 48.8 |
| Thorium-232 | Lead-208 | 14.0 |
| Uranium-238 | Lead-206 | 4.47 |
| Potassium-40 | Argon-40 | 1.31 |

> **Fun fact:** Uranium-238's half-life (4.47 billion years) is almost exactly the age of Earth!

## 4. Earth's Magnetic Shield

Earth has a strong, global magnetic field shaped like a **dipole**, like a bar magnet with a **north and south pole**.

### How It Works (The Geodynamo)

- Earth's **outer core is liquid iron**.
- Heat makes that liquid **convect** (hot material rises, cooler material sinks).
- This steady motion of liquid metal **generates the magnetic field**.

### Why Not Every Planet Has One

Uranus and Neptune have interiors that are split into **unmixed layers that do not convect**. They have **no dipole field**, only a messy, disorganized one.

## 5. Air, Weather, and a Young Surface

### The Five Layers of the Atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere sits right on top of solid ground, and it is split into layers by **temperature changes**.

| # | Layer | What happens here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Troposphere** | Lowest layer. **Weather, clouds, and convection.** Temperature **drops** as you go up. |
| 2 | **Stratosphere** | Above the troposphere. Temperature **rises** with height. |
| 3 | **Mesosphere** | Middle-upper region. (Jupiter has no mesosphere.) |
| 4 | **Thermosphere** | Very hot and energetic. **Auroras** and airglow happen here. |
| 5 | **Exosphere** | Outermost layer. Air thins out until it fades into space. |

### Global Air Circulation

The equator gets more sunlight than the poles. This imbalance pushes air in giant north-south loops called **circulation cells**. Each hemisphere has **three**:

1. **Hadley cell**: near the equator (tropics)
2. **Ferrel cell**: mid-latitudes
3. **Polar cell**: near the poles

In the Ferrel cell, the low-level return flow is balanced by friction in the **Ekman layer**.

### A Planet That Cleans Itself

Earth is **4.5 billion years old**, but its **surface is geologically very young**. Why?

- **Volcanism**: lava floods cover old ground.
- **Erosion**: water and ice wear down and move rock. This is called **planet weathering**.

Together, these processes **sweep the surface clean** and erase **impact craters**.

| | Earth | The Moon |
|---|---|---|
| Geologically active? | Yes | No ("dead") |
| Craters | Mostly erased | Preserved for billions of years |
| Surface age | Young | Ancient |

## 6. Why Earth Supports Life

Earth is the **only body in the solar system confirmed to have life**. Three things come together.

### 1. The Right Distance

Sunlight weakens with the **square of the distance** from the Sun. Earth is far enough to avoid being scorched and close enough to avoid being frozen.

### 2. The Greenhouse Blanket

Earth's atmosphere **traps heat** like a blanket. Without it, surface temperatures would plunge and the oceans would **freeze solid**.

### 3. Liquid Water

Earth's surface temperatures mostly sit **between water's freezing and boiling points**:

| | Celsius | Kelvin |
|---|---|---|
| Freezing | 0 °C | 273 K |
| Boiling | 100 °C | 373 K |

That keeps **liquid oceans** stable.

### Making the Ingredients of Life: Miller-Urey (1953)

Scientists wanted to know: could the building blocks of life form from simple gases on early Earth?

- **Ingredients (simulated early atmosphere):**
  - Water vapor (H₂O)
  - Methane (CH₄)
  - Ammonia (NH₃)
  - Hydrogen (H₂)
- **Energy:** electric **sparks**, standing in for **lightning**.
- **Result:** the mixture produced **organic matter, including amino acids**, the building blocks of proteins.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Terrestrial planet** | Dense, rocky planet with a solid surface (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) |
| **Oxidized chemistry** | Rocks dominated by oxygen compounds like silicates |
| **Differentiation** | Sorting of a molten planet into layers by density |
| **Dipole field** | Magnetic field with two opposite poles |
| **Geodynamo** | Convection in the liquid outer core that creates the magnetic field |
| **Sidereal month** | Moon's orbit measured against the stars (27.3 days) |
| **Synodic month** | Moon's phase cycle measured against the Sun (29.53 days) |
| **Planet weathering** | Water, ice, and volcanism resurfacing a planet |
| **Half-life** | Time for half of a radioactive sample to decay |
